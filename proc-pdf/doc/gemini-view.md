Forensic Fiduciary Audit of NIMCRUT Account 1603387: A Quantitative Performance Analysis and Suitability Review (Q4-2023 to Q4-2025)The administration of fiduciary accounts requires an unwavering adherence to the principles of prudence, loyalty, and impartiality, particularly when managing complex structures such as a Net Income with Makeup Charitable Remainder Unitrust (NIMCRUT). Account 1603387, throughout the period spanning from the fourth quarter of 2023 to the fourth quarter of 2025, presents a critical case study in the intersection of high-cost proprietary fund selection, the potential for reverse churning, and the misapplication of tax-exempt securities within a tax-exempt trust shell. This report provides an exhaustive quantitative and qualitative analysis of the investment strategies employed in this account, evaluating the actual performance of SEI Institutional Managed Trust (SIMT) funds against optimal benchmark alternatives, hereafter referred to as the 'roads not taken.'The Regulatory and Statutory Landscape of Fiduciary Duty in MichiganThe legal framework governing the administration of account 1603387 is rooted in the Michigan Trust Code and the Estates and Protected Individuals Code (EPIC), which establish rigorous standards for fiduciaries. Under Michigan Compiled Laws (MCL) 700.1308, any violation by a fiduciary of a duty owed to an heir, devisee, or beneficiary—including the charitable remainder interest—constitutes a breach of duty. To remedy such a breach, Michigan courts possess broad authority to compel the fiduciary to perform their duties, enjoin them from further breaches, and most critically, compel the redress of a breach through the payment of money or the restoration of property.The duty of loyalty, codified in MCL 700.7802, mandates that a trustee administer a trust solely in the interests of the trust beneficiaries. This duty is fundamentally compromised when a trustee engages in self-dealing or enters into transactions that create a conflict of interest, such as the selection of proprietary investment products that generate undisclosed revenue for the trustee or its affiliates. Furthermore, the Michigan Prudent Investor Rule requires that a fiduciary manage trust assets as a prudent investor would, considering the trust's purposes, terms, and distribution requirements. This involves the application of reasonable care, skill, and caution to the portfolio as a whole, rather than viewing individual investments in isolation.In the context of a NIMCRUT, the fiduciary also owes a distinct duty of impartiality. As outlined in MCL 700.7803, if a trust has two or more beneficiaries, the trustee must act impartially in investing, managing, and distributing the trust property, giving due regard to the beneficiaries' respective interests. In account 1603387, the tension between the income beneficiary’s need for distributable Trust Accounting Income (TAI) and the remainder charity’s interest in long-term capital appreciation requires a sophisticated balancing act that, as the following analysis suggests, was neglected in favor of high-cost, underperforming proprietary vehicles.Structural Mechanics of NIMCRUT Distributions and the Impact of Asset SelectionA NIMCRUT is a specialized charitable vehicle that pays the income beneficiary the lesser of a fixed unitrust percentage or the trust's actual net income (TAI). The 'makeup' provision (the 'M' in NIMCRUT) allows for the distribution of excess income in future years to compensate for years in which the net income was less than the unitrust amount. Consequently, the definition of what constitutes 'income' for trust accounting purposes—as opposed to taxable income—is the fulcrum upon which the beneficiary's cash flow turns.Under the Michigan Uniform Principal and Income Act (UPIA), the characterization of receipts as income or principal is generally determined by the trust instrument or, in its absence, by default statutory rules. A critical fiduciary failure in account 1603387 was the selection of assets that systematically suppressed TAI through high internal expenses and the use of 'tax-managed' strategies that prioritize capital appreciation over yield. Under FINRA Rule 2111 and the SEC's Regulation Best Interest (Reg BI), fiduciaries must ensure that recommendations are suitable for the client's tax status.A charitable remainder trust is a tax-exempt entity under Internal Revenue Code Section 664. Therefore, it derives zero benefit from the federal income tax exemption offered by municipal bonds. Because municipal bonds pay lower interest rates than taxable bonds (due to their tax-advantaged status for individual investors), investing in them inside a tax-exempt trust is a purely detrimental act.The mathematical loss can be modeled using the Tax-Equivalent Yield (TEY) formula:$$TEY = \frac{Tax-Free\ Yield}{1 - Marginal\ Tax\ Rate}$$For the NIMCRUT, where the marginal tax rate is 0%, the $TEY$ is simply the tax-free yield. A prudent investor would have sought the highest taxable yield, which for a similar risk profile (VMFXX), was significantly higher. The choice of TFCAX effectively prioritized a tax benefit that the client could not use, while sacrificing the yield the client desperately needed to meet their unitrust payout.
